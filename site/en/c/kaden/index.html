Appliances and furniture

30 drawings in Appliances and furniture, each generated from a single YAML file.

  • Fitting a refrigerator: clearances differ by model, and a corner turns on the diagonal
  • A bench built for making: tenons mitred where they meet, and a top that is never screwed down
  • A chair drawn for making: four tenons meet in the back leg, and they must be set at different heights
  • A fridge does not make cold, it pumps heat out — and it throws away more than it took, because the compressor's electricity comes out as heat too
  • Heating and cooling swap the roles of the indoor and outdoor coils, and the only part that changes is one valve — which is also why a heat pump briefly runs cooling in the middle of heating, to melt its own frost
  • Hot and cold spots sit 6.1cm apart because that is half of the 12.2cm wavelength, which no cabinet design can remove — so the food is moved instead
  • A shelf sags with the cube of its span, so doubling the span sags it eight times — and gluing a 40mm rail under the front edge does more than upgrading the board
  • The same drum has to stay under 1G to wash and reach 335G to spin, which is why it hangs on springs behind a concrete weight rather than being bolted to the machine
  • Induction heats only the outer 0.05mm of the pan, and that thin skin is why iron works and aluminium does not: at 20kHz iron's surface resistance is 43 times aluminium's
  • Centrifugal force is v squared over r, so a tenth of the radius is ten times the g — but a tenth of the radius is a hundredth of the airflow, which is why the small cones come in rows
  • The slide decides the drawer, not the other way round: a side-mounted pair takes 12.7mm each side, so a 500mm opening leaves a 474.6mm box
  • A water heater is rated in heat, not flow: the same 24-go unit gives 40 litres a minute in summer and 17 in winter, because the rating assumes a 25 degree rise
  • Boiling water refuses to go past 100C, so a pressure cooker adds exactly one part — a valve — to lift that ceiling to about 105C
  • A dishwasher does not use less water, it uses the same water many times: 10 litres pumped around twenty times is 200 litres of water hitting the dishes
  • The airflow on the box is the free-delivery figure; what you actually get is where the fan curve meets the duct resistance, and a long bent duct can cut it to 43 percent
  • An LCD makes no light of its own — it only blocks it, which is why its black is whatever light failed to be blocked, and why only a tenth of the backlight ever reaches your eye
  • Mass cancels out of the tipping condition, so how easily furniture falls depends only on its depth and the height of its centre of gravity — a shallow tall bookcase goes over at a shindo 5+
  • You cannot size a cabinet door until the hinge is chosen: the overlay goes straight into the width, and full overlay versus inset differs by 18mm per door
  • Chaining power strips does not add up: the wall socket caps the whole chain at 15A, the flex gauge caps it lower still (0.75sq means 7A), and the 20A branch breaker trips after the 15A strip has already overheated
  • Dovetail slope is the limit before the corner grain snaps (1:6 in softwood, 1:8 in hardwood), a tenon is one third of the stock, and a 600mm top moves 9mm across the grain — so the joint that fails is usually the one that was not allowed to move
  • Every humidifier needs the same 627Wh to evaporate a litre — the four types differ only in where that heat comes from, which is why the evaporative one is cheap on the meter and cools the room instead
  • The lumens on the box describe the fitting, not the desk: a polar candela plot is the only document that connects them, and at 2.5m a spot that gives 400lx underneath gives 110lx just 1.4m to the side
  • The single arc on a floor plan decides how the room can be used: a 780mm frame only gives 747mm of clear opening once the leaf is in the way, and the door sweeps 0.44m² of floor that nothing else can occupy
  • Ankle taping stops one direction only — inversion — and leaves the up-and-down movement alone; the order matters because each strip pins down the ends of the last, so tape that peels is usually tape applied in the wrong order
  • A spider does not stick to its own web because only the spiral is sticky: the radials and the temporary scaffold are dry, and the scaffold is cut away as the sticky spiral is laid from the outside in
  • A population pyramid is not a picture of today but eighty years of births stacked upright: the bulges and the notch move up one step every year, and the notch at 1966 records a year people avoided, not a generation that was lost
  • Kumiko lattice is arithmetic before it is woodwork: fixing the hexagon fixes every length and angle in the pattern, and the three-way joint only lies flat because the three pieces are notched to different depths — one third, two thirds, one half
  • A haircut diagram records the angle the hair was held at, not the millimetres taken off: 0 degrees gives one length, 90 degrees gives a uniform layer, and everything in between is graduation
  • A cadastral map is not a drawing but a set of coordinates: the boundary points are held as plane rectangular coordinates, the lines merely join them, and the area falls out of the arithmetic rather than a tape measure
  • The order of the filters is the order of their lifetimes, and the room size on the box is really a time: it names the room this machine can clean in thirty minutes, so a room its own size takes the full half hour
