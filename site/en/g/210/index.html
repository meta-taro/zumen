楽譜の寸法（五線が単位）

Music engraving: the staff space is the unit for every measurement, and the stem is 3.5 of them

This drawing comes from one YAML file. No shape was moved by hand.

What the source says

五線の間隔（1 space）が、すべての寸法の単位。

符頭は縦 1 × 横 1.18 space、符尾は 3.5 space（＝1 オクターブ）、連桁は太さ 0.5 space、五線の線は 0.125 space。

符尾の向きは真ん中の線で決まり（上なら下向き、下なら上向き）、付く側も決まっている（上向きは符頭の右、下向きは左）。♯を置く順はファ・ド・ソ・レ・ラ・ミ・シで、置く場所まで決まっている。

  • Guitar frets are a geometric series, and the saddle must still be moved back to play in tune
  • Hall acoustics: delay is nothing but a difference in path length — one millisecond is 0.34 m
  • A piano action: the jack lets go just before the string, so nothing is pushing the hammer at impact
  • Frets crowd together as you climb because what is divided into twelve is not a distance but a ratio: each semitone multiplies by 2^(1/12), so the twelfth fret lands at exactly half the string and everything beyond it repeats at half size
  • The black keys are grouped in twos and threes so that a hand can find its place without looking — twelve evenly spaced keys would give no landmark at all — and 88 turns out to be seven octaves plus four: three keys added below and one above
  • The strings run left to right from high to low and the louder the instrument the further back it sits, so the seating plan is itself a balance control — and "double wind" or "triple wind" names the size of the orchestra by counting only the woodwind
  • A polar plot of how much a microphone picks up from each direction. The three familiar shapes are not separate inventions but one formula at its ends and middle — omnidirectional, cardioid and figure-of-eight are A = 1, 0.5 and 0 in r = A + (1 - A)cos θ. Solving the same formula gives the dead angles: 180 degrees for a cardioid, 90 and 270 for a figure-of-eight.
  • The same control means different things depending on where in the chain it sits. An aux send taken before the fader keeps its level when the engineer moves the fader, which is what a performer monitor needs; taken after, it follows the fader, which is what a reverb needs. Insert is in series and cuts the path open; aux is in parallel and branches away and back.
